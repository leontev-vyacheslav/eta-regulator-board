A **temperature graph of a heating system** (also known as a **heating curve**) shows how the **supply temperature** of the heating system (e.g., water temperature in radiators or underfloor heating) varies depending on the **outside temperature**. This is a key concept in modern heating control systems, particularly in **weather-compensated heating systems**.

### **Key Features of a Heating Curve Graph:**
1. **X-axis (Horizontal):** Outside temperature (e.g., from +20°C to -20°C)
2. **Y-axis (Vertical):** Heating system supply temperature (e.g., from 20°C to 80°C)
3. **Curve Shape:** Typically a **downward-sloping line**, meaning as outside temperature drops, the heating system increases supply temperature.

### **How It Works:**
- When it’s **cold outside**, the heating system increases water temperature to compensate for heat loss.
- When it’s **warmer outside**, the system reduces water temperature to avoid overheating and save energy.

### **Example Heating Curve Values:**
| Outside Temp. (°C) | Supply Temp. (°C) |
|--------------------|-------------------|
| +10°C              | 35°C              |
| 0°C                | 45°C              |
| -10°C              | 60°C              |
| -20°C              | 75°C              |

### **Adjustable Parameters:**
- **Slope (Steepness):** Defines how sharply the supply temperature increases as it gets colder.
- **Parallel Shift:** Moves the entire curve up (higher supply temp) or down (lower supply temp).
- **Minimum & Maximum Limits:** Ensures the system never exceeds safe temperatures.

### **Applications:**
- **Hydronic heating systems** (radiators, underfloor heating).
- **Heat pumps** (optimizes efficiency by avoiding extreme temperatures).
- **Condensing boilers** (works best at lower return temperatures).

### **Benefits of Using a Heating Curve:**
✔ **Energy efficiency** (avovers overheating).
✔ **Improved comfort** (avoids temperature swings).
✔ **Longer system lifespan** (reduces wear and tear).
